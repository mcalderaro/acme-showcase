ACME ClawWorks is founder-led on purpose.

The public operating model only makes sense if visitors can understand who decides what, which roles own execution, and where review gates apply.

## Human authority stays visible

Matt Calderaro is ACME’s CTO and the final human authority in the system.

That means Matt is the person who:

- initiates new work
- sets direction and priorities
- approves major changes and milestone outcomes
- approves public publishing

Agents help execute the work. They do not replace executive authority.

## Role ownership across the company

ACME uses specialist roles so requests do not collapse into one undifferentiated stream of agent activity.

The current public role model is:

- **Matt Calderaro** — CTO and final approver
- **Alex Mercer** — Program Director and coordination owner
- **Emma Carter** — Product Manager
- **Adrian Cole** — Principal Software Architect
- **Ethan Brooks** — Senior Software Engineer
- **Don White** — QA Automation Engineer
- **Nora Hale** — Technical Documentation & Showcase Content Manager
- **Victor Chen** — Finance & Cost Intelligence

Each role exists to preserve traceability. Product defines intent, architecture defines form, engineering builds, QA validates, documentation explains, and finance measures.

## Approval model

Not every action needs the same level of approval.

Routine execution can move within specialist ownership. But several categories stay explicitly gated:

- new product creation
- major architecture changes
- milestone approval
- public publishing

That approval structure keeps ACME fast without making it blurry.

## Systems of record

ACME keeps authority grounded in systems of record rather than in vague conversational memory.

Publicly, the most important systems are:

- **Plane** for tracked work, workflow state, and milestone grouping
- **GitHub** for branches, commits, pull requests, and published documentation

Agents operate on top of those systems. They do not bypass them.

## Why this matters publicly

The public showcase is meant to be credible, not just polished.

That credibility comes from visible governance:

- requests become tracked work
- ownership is named
- approvals are explicit
- public updates are reviewable before publication

In other words, the governance model is part of the product story.
