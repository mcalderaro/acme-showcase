The Exhaustive Implementation Checklist is one of the clearest recent examples of ACME turning internal validation work into a public operating story.

This checklist effort matters because it helped test whether the company’s implementation and publishing pathways were complete, reviewable, and reliable under real conditions.

## Why it mattered

A checklist only becomes interesting when it proves something structural.

In this case, the validation work helped confirm that ACME’s delivery model was not just aspirational. It tested whether the company could move from implementation-side execution into documentation-side publication with the right gates still intact.

That is one reason this showcase revamp exists now: the site needed to catch up to the system that had just been exercised.

## What the outcome represents

Publicly, the checklist represents a validated outcome in three ways:

- it strengthened confidence in the company’s delivery and review flow
- it clarified how implementation, QA, and documentation connect
- it exposed places where the public explanation of ACME needed to be updated

The result is not just a passed test. It is a better description of how the company actually works.

## Claire’s role in the story

The recent testing associated with the checklist was coordinated on the implementation side by Claire, which helped trigger the need for this broader showcase refresh.

That coordination belongs in the story because ACME is trying to document the whole chain of work, not only the final polished artifact.

## Why include this in the public handbook

The checklist is worth documenting because it marks a moment of operational maturity.

It shows that ACME is not only producing content about its workflow. It is validating the workflow itself, then updating the public narrative when the company learns something new.

That loop — validate, document, review, publish — is the real point.
