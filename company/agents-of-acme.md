ACME ClawWorks is powered by a human-led, AI-assisted operating team.

These agents are not presented as magic. They are specialists with defined roles inside a real workflow, working under human direction and within clear boundaries.

## Why the agents matter

The point of ACME is not just that software gets built.

The point is that the company itself is legible: who is responsible for what, how work moves, and where human judgment remains in charge.

The agents are part of that operating model.

## The team at a glance

### Matt Calderaro — CTO (Human)

Matt sets company direction, approves major decisions, and provides the human oversight that keeps the system grounded.

### Alex Mercer — Program Director

Alex coordinates operations across the company. Work intake, queue movement, milestone flow, and cross-team coordination all run through this role.

### Emma Carter — Product Manager

Emma helps shape work into clear product direction. That includes turning rough requests into something scoped, understandable, and ready to move forward.

### Adrian Cole — Principal Software Architect

Adrian defines technical guardrails, architecture decisions, and reusable system patterns so products are built on purpose instead of by accident.

### Ethan Brooks — Senior Software Engineer

Ethan turns approved plans into working software, shared components, and implementation progress.

### Don White — QA Automation Engineer

Don focuses on validation, regression checking, and making sure the work behaves the way the team says it should.

### Nora Hale — Technical Documentation & Showcase Content Manager

Nora documents milestones, shapes internal knowledge into readable public stories, and manages the public-facing showcase content.

### Victor Chen — Finance & Cost Intelligence

Victor tracks model usage, cost visibility, and the economics of operating an AI-assisted software company.

## How these roles work together

The agents are not interchangeable generalists.

Each role exists to keep the company understandable:

- operations keeps work moving
- product clarifies intent
- architecture defines constraints
- engineering builds
- QA validates
- documentation explains
- finance measures cost and efficiency

That structure makes it possible to build in public without turning the company into improvisation.

## Human-led, not human-absent

ACME is intentionally human-led.

The agents do real work, but they do not replace executive direction, approval, or judgment. Matt remains the person who sets strategy, approves important outcomes, and decides what becomes real.

That is part of the experiment too: not whether AI can run unsupervised, but whether a company can become more transparent, more organized, and more effective when AI specialists are embedded into its daily operations.

## Why show the team publicly

Because the team structure explains the work.

When you see a milestone, a dev log, or a company explainer on this site, you are seeing the output of a system with named roles, visible handoffs, and documented responsibility.

The agents of ACME are not background decoration. They are the working cast of the studio.
