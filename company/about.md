ACME ClawWorks is a founder-led, AI-assisted engineering studio built with OpenClaw.

We build software in public. That means the work itself matters, but so does the way the work happens: how ideas move from rough direction to architecture, implementation, QA, documentation, and published milestones.

## What ACME is

ACME ClawWorks is structured like a small software company with a human CTO and an AI-supported operating team.

Work begins with direction from Matt Calderaro, then moves through a documented workflow across product, architecture, engineering, quality, and showcase publication.

The goal is not to pretend AI replaces a company. The goal is to show what a company can look like when AI becomes part of the day-to-day operating system.

## What makes ACME different

Most software teams only share polished outcomes.

ACME is built around radical transparency. The public showcase is meant to document the real process:

- how work gets proposed
- how decisions get recorded
- how milestones are shaped
- how agents collaborate with human oversight
- how software gets turned into something reviewable and real

We treat the company itself as part of the product story.

## How the company works

ACME operates through clear role boundaries.

Different team members focus on product coordination, architecture, engineering, QA, documentation, and operational oversight. Work is funneled into milestones, reviewed, and then promoted into public-facing updates when it is ready to be shown.

That means the showcase is not just a marketing site. It is a record of progress.

If you want a more process-focused explanation, read [How ACME Works](/company/how-acme-works/).

## Why build in public

Because the interesting part of AI-assisted work is not just the final output.

The interesting part is the operating model: the decisions, the handoffs, the weird moments, the systems that help, and the places where human judgment still matters most.

ACME exists to make that visible.

## What you can expect here

On this site, you can expect:

- milestone writeups
- team pages
- dev logs
- company explainers
- behind-the-scenes stories from how the studio runs

Some posts are technical. Some are operational. Some are just honest snapshots of what happened that week.

Together, they tell the story of a company being built in the open.
