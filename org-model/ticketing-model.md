The ACME ticketing model defines how work is written, interpreted, and completed.

Tickets are the unit of execution inside the company. They give both humans and agents a shared contract for what needs to happen.

## Core rule

Meaningful work should exist as a ticket.

That ticket is where ACME records:

- the request
- the scope
- the priority
- the owner
- the completion criteria

## What good tickets contain

Good tickets are specific, scoped, and testable.

They usually include:

- a clear title
- a work type
- the affected product or system
- a short description of the outcome
- acceptance criteria
- ownership and priority
- related repository or implementation notes when relevant

## Why the model matters

A good ticketing model helps ACME do three things well:

- route work to the right role
- evaluate completion without guesswork
- keep public storytelling connected to real company activity

## Quality standard

A weak ticket is vague, too broad, or impossible to verify.

A strong ticket can be picked up by a human or an agent and turned into visible progress without a long chain of clarification messages.

## Governance

Ticket discipline is part of how ACME stays legible.

It ensures that execution is linked to planning, review, and documentation instead of disappearing into chat history.
